How to read this dashboard
🟥
Anomalies (the math) — days where Boeing's stock moved drastically differently from the rest of the aerospace sector — an "idiosyncratic" move that market-wide news can't explain. Three independent detectors vote; 2+ votes raises a flag.
🔶
Events (the reality) — curated dates of real, known Boeing incidents (737 MAX groundings, the door-plug blowout, DOJ charges…). We use these to grade the detector: did the math catch the real event?
📈
Flare probability (the forecast) — the model's estimate that Boeing will have an abnormal move within the next 5 days. This is the "predict the market" part: it forecasts risk (how bumpy), not direction (up vs down) — direction is honestly near a coin flip.
💵
Risk management (the payoff) — what happens to $10k if you simply step to cash for 5 days after each anomaly flag, vs holding through everything. Fewer deep losses is the goal.

Risk management — did the alarm actually save money?

Imagine you invested $10,000 in Boeing in 2016. The green line sells to cash for 5 days every time the alarm rings, then buys back in. The grey line ignores the alarm and just holds. If green stays above grey, the alarm is earning its keep.

How is this graph made?
1. Every day since 2016 we re-run the anomaly detector using only information available up to that day (no hindsight).
2. When 2 of 3 detectors flag a day as abnormal, the strategy sells Boeing at the next day's price and sits in cash for 5 trading days, then buys back.
3. We subtract realistic trading costs (0.05% per trade) from every buy/sell.
4. Both lines compound the same $10,000 forward day by day; the drawdown chart is each line's distance below its own all-time high.
Source data: daily Boeing prices from Yahoo Finance. Code: src/strategy.py in the GitHub repo.

Industry health check — how is Boeing's world doing?

Three real-world gauges of Boeing's business environment: are people flying? Is Boeing making money? Is the news good or bad? These give context to the alarms above (they're shown for information — our tests found they don't improve the math model itself).

Are people flying? Airport traffic vs the same day last year. Above 0% = more people flying than a year ago (good for airplane orders); below 0% = travel is shrinking (bad for Boeing's customers).

Is the news good or bad? Each day's Boeing headlines are read by an AI and scored from −1 (very negative: crashes, probes, lawsuits) to +1 (very positive: orders, deliveries, upgrades). Sustained dips below zero mean a rough news cycle.

How are these graphs made?
Are people flying? (TSA demand) — every day the TSA publishes how many passengers went through US airport security. We fetch that table (tsa.gov + a maintained mirror), then compare each day to the same day last year. Code: src/demand.py.
Boeing's finances (SEC cards above) — revenue and operating cash flow come straight from Boeing's official quarterly filings via the SEC EDGAR API. Each number only appears on the dashboard from the date it was actually filed — no peeking at the future. Code: src/edgar.py.
News sentiment — we pull Boeing headlines from free Yahoo Finance and Google News RSS feeds, then a finance-tuned AI language model (FinBERT) reads each headline and scores it from −1 (negative) to +1 (positive). Daily average is plotted. Code: src/news.py.
